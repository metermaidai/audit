# Agent Waste Index — method, caveats, retractions

Companion to [index.md](index.md). The tables there are regenerated by `python pipeline.py report`;
this file is written by hand and is where anything that needs saying in prose lives.

## Edition 1

341,054 runs, 11 public trajectory datasets, 29 dataset/model/scaffold groups, 16 model labels,
4 scaffold families (openhands, swe-agent, mini-swe-agent, terminus-2). Every ingested run was
parsed and scored by `pipeline.py` at ingest; nothing was sampled after the fact.

Ingest is capped at the first 20,000 rows of each dataset/config/split (`sweep --limit 20000`),
taken in the dataset's own streaming order. For the larger sources — `mini-coder-trajs-400k`,
`AlienKevin/SWE-ZERO-12M-trajectories` — that is a head sample, not the whole dataset, and it
inherits whatever ordering the publisher used. The cap applies per dataset/config/split, and the
model rows in the tables are subdivisions of that: the three SWE-smith splits each sum to exactly
20,000 runs across their model rows, and the three SWE-agent-trajectories model rows sum to 19,999
(one row failed to parse). A group well under the cap — OpenHands-Sampled-Trajectories at 6,055
across both models — exhausted its split.

## Cost basis

No public trajectory dataset in edition 1 carries per-run cost, so cost is **estimated**:
characters/4 as tokens, priced at Sonnet-class rates, with input re-sent every step (the naive
"whole context each turn" model). Two variants are reported — no cache, and cached reads at 10%
of input — because prompt caching moves the totals materially.

**Quote the percentages, not the dollars.** The dollar column is a common yardstick across
datasets, not a claim about what anyone actually paid. Groups also differ in task mix, so
resolve rates are comparable *within* a group, not across groups. SWE-smith in particular is a
curated training set; its resolve rate is not a benchmark score.

## What counts as waste

Mechanical waste counts three detectors:

- **loop** — identical action three or more times with no state-changing action in between
- **blind retry** — identical action repeated immediately after an error observation
- **context bloat** — an observation over 20,000 characters, priced for the excess dragged
  through every remaining step

**Edit thrash** (the same file edited six or more times) is reported per-group but is *not*
counted in mechanical waste and *not* counted in "any finding". It fires on over 30% of runs in
20 of the 29 groups, peaking at 78% — a rate that makes it a description of how a scaffold edits
files rather than a detection of waste, since the detector cannot tell a productive
edit-test-edit cycle from thrashing. It stays in the tables as a signal, not as a cost.

Runs that ended without a real result (context exhausted, abandoned, cut off) are tracked
separately from mechanical waste and never added to it — the whole run's cost is at stake there,
not a few steps.

`trajectory_audit.py` uses the same three-detector basis, so a local audit is comparable to the
Index. If you change the detector set in one, change it in the other.

## Model attribution

`nvidia/Open-SWE-Traces` does not attribute a single generating model per config on its dataset
card, and the traces carry no model field. Those rows are labelled by trace version
("Open-SWE v1.0 (see card)") rather than by a model name. Do not substitute a guess: three
configs and 100,000 runs ride on that label.

Dataset-provenance labels are also not model names. `nebius-swe-rebench-openhands`,
`swe-smith-claude-3-7-sonnet` and `kwai-klear-swe-smith-mini` are the source labels
`thoughtworks/agentic-coding-trajectories` carries for its own rows; they overlap with other
datasets in the set. The `nebius-swe-rebench-openhands` group matches
`nebius/SWE-rebench-openhands-trajectories` to within sampling noise on every statistic
(median steps 61 vs 61, bloat 72.0% vs 72.3%, thrash 68.8% vs 69.3%, ended-without-result
9.4% vs 9.5%, resolve 48.6% vs 47.9%) — it is a 5,000-run resample of the same population, and
`swe-smith-claude-3-7-sonnet` sits close to the SWE-smith Claude 3.7 groups. Read those two as
replication, not as extra independent evidence, and do not pool them with their sources.

## Retractions

**"Waste predicts failure everywhere" — retracted.** The 4,000-run pilot reported that runs
tripping any detector resolved at roughly half the rate of clean runs, in all five model groups
it covered, and framed that as a general mid-run kill signal. At 341,054 runs it does not
generalise:

- Of the 20 groups with resolve labels, 15 resolve worse when flagged and 5 resolve *better*.
- The effect is large only in the weak-model groups: swe-agent-llama-8b 0.16×, llama-405b 0.19×,
  llama-70b 0.21×, gpt-4o/OpenHands 0.25×.
- It reverses in the SWE-smith Claude 3.5 Sonnet groups (1.10×, 1.21×, 1.22×) and in
  mini-coder-trajs-400k (1.18×).

What survives is narrower and worth stating plainly: a tripped detector is a strong failure
signal on weak models, and close to no signal at the frontier. Do not ship a mid-run kill switch
on this basis without measuring it on your own traffic first.

**Pilot dollar figures — withdrawn.** The pilot quoted "$8–9 per failed Claude 3.7 run" and
"22% of Claude 3.7 spend on runs that produced nothing". Both came from a 2,000-run slice of
SWE-smith with no exit status, where "produced nothing" was a no-submit heuristic. With the
full split parsed and end-of-run rules applied, SWE-smith Claude 3.7 runs end submitted or with
a closing message, and the failed-run share for those groups is 0%. The per-run dollar figures
were estimates on the basis above and should not be quoted at all.

## Known limits

- Detectors are syntactic. A loop is identical action text; a semantically identical action
  phrased two ways is not caught.
- `resolved` is present for 20 of 29 groups. Groups without it show `—` and are excluded from
  every resolve statistic rather than treated as unresolved.
- The step-quintile tables require at least 200 runs per bucket, so small groups drop out.
- The SWE-bench leaderboard S3 bucket and Princeton HAL traces are access-restricted and are not
  in edition 1.
